# Write with judgment

Help the writing do its job for the reader. Diagnose what gets in the way,
revise within the brief, and check what changed in meaning and effect. Preserve
what already works.

## 1. Infer the job

Read the request and the whole relevant passage. Infer audience, purpose, genre,
English variety, venue rules, length, and permitted edit from context. Ask only
when an open choice would materially change the result; continue the unambiguous
work.

| Request | Permitted edit |
| --- | --- |
| Proofread, copyedit, light polish | Correct errors and improve readability; preserve claims and effective voice, and structure where practical. |
| Rewrite, improve, strengthen | Reorganize and rephrase; preserve position and evidential strength. |
| Review, critique | Diagnose problems and propose repairs; rewrite only when requested. |
| Substantive revision | Repair the argument within the evidence; disclose changed claims and missing support. |
| Shorten, summarize, adapt | Select for the purpose; preserve decisive qualifications and retained claims' scope. |
| Draft from a brief | Use supplied or verified material; invent only where a creative brief permits it. |

Separate **must survive**, **may change**, and **needs verification**. "Stronger"
does not mean more certain; "concrete" does not permit invented facts. Mark gaps
and unsupported claims.

Protect required meaning, quotations, literal identifiers, and intended effect.
Keep each citation with the claim it supports; flag cuts that remove cited
claims. Apply the user's instructions and required venue style without silently
changing a claim. English variety, citation style, and prose conventions are
independent choices.

## 2. Load the genre guidance

For substantial or genre-specific work, read the relevant guidance; mixed
documents may need several references. Handle self-contained corrections directly.

- [scientific.md](references/scientific.md): manuscripts, grants, reviews, and
  response letters; architecture, section jobs, and claim calibration.
- [technical.md](references/technical.md): documentation, procedures,
  specifications, tool descriptions, prompts, and error or status messages.
- [genres.md](references/genres.md): essays, letters and reports, fiction and
  poetry, summaries.

## 3. Diagnose, then revise

Identify the main obstacle: missing premise, buried point, ambiguous actor,
misplaced condition, unstable terminology, needless abstraction, redundancy,
wrong tone, or broken rhythm. Fix the highest-level problem first. For substantial
prose, check each paragraph's job and progression. Preserve effective voice:
diction, rhythm, restraint, humour, or the way an idea unfolds.

- Keep conditions beside the actions they govern and qualifications beside the
  claims they limit.
- Keep technical referents stable; preserve expressive variation when it works.
- Judge economy by reader effort, not word count.
- Treat familiar style advice as conditional.

When rules conflict or a paragraph will not cohere, read
[decisions.md](references/decisions.md). For light polish, or when unsure whether
to intervene at all, read [examples.md](references/examples.md), which includes
deliberate non-edits.

For a debatable edit, ask: **if I reverted this, what would the reader lose?**
Clarity, economy, rhythm, or a requested change of style can justify an edit.
If the benefit is only your preference, keep the original. Large problems justify
large changes within the brief. Treat a required length as a constraint;
word counts, passive counts, readability grades, and banned-word lists do not
establish quality.

## 4. Verify separately

In a separate pass, compare roles, logic, certainty or obligation, scope,
numbers, and citation attachment. For faithful edits, check for added or lost
meaning. For summaries, check support for retained claims and coverage of the
essentials; for substantive revisions or drafts, check against the brief and
evidence. Compare relationships, not just repeated words. When a faithful edit's
equivalence is uncertain, ask whether either version could be true while the
other is false.

When revising claims, conditions, numbers, or instructions that someone will
publish or act on, read
[verification.md](references/verification.md), which also covers the optional
`scripts/compare_anchors.py` check and when to use a fresh reviewer. Stop when the
remaining changes are matters of taste.

## 5. Deliver

For review, lead with consequential findings tied to passages. For drafting or
revision, lead with usable text; when asked to edit repository files, make those
edits and report the result. Add a brief note for a substantive change, unresolved
ambiguity, missing evidence, or a cut whose omissions the user should assess.
When explaining edits, distinguish corrections, house-style choices, suggestions,
and substantive changes. If good writing needs no change, say why in one line.

Maintain a [style sheet](references/style-sheet.md) for recurring projects when
authorized. For disputed conventions, consult
[sources.md](references/sources.md) and current primary guidance. Never claim
comprehensive Chicago or certified ASD-STE100 compliance.
