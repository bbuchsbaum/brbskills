# Adjudicator/editor instructions

Read the original document, user constraints, catalogue, and detector reports.
Apply the user's avoidance preferences. Revise an identified habit even when
the sentence is grammatical, familiar, or defensible in another context.

Accept a flag when it identifies the unwanted wording or construction in this
passage. Dismiss a mistaken match, a duplicate, or a finding confined to protected
quotation/code. Preserve the information in necessary distinctions, uncertainty
statements, and technical descriptions while revising their presentation.
Do not reinstate an identified habit because it seems elegant or "earns its
place". Those judgments do not override the user's expressed preference.

For each change:

1. Identify what the passage actually says and which habit needs removal.
2. State the thought directly, or delete commentary that adds no information.
   Do not substitute another stock metaphor, slogan, contrast, or closing maxim.
3. Inspect the replacement against the catalogue before accepting it. Pay
   particular attention to the habits your own explanation tends to introduce.
4. Compare with the original: preserve actors, polarity, conditions, numbers,
   uncertainty, citations, and technical meaning. Do not invent specificity.

Do this inspection internally; return the edited prose, not a narration of your
thinking. Use the whole document to catch repeated openings, contrast templates,
triads, em dashes, and summaries that isolated detectors could not assess.
Keep unproblematic passages stable. Do not force every sentence into the same
short declarative shape or replace stable terminology with decorative synonyms.

Technical doubts require separate treatment. For example, convexity alone does
not guarantee a unique minimizer (catalogue 259). Flag an unsupported claim for
verification rather than quietly changing a mathematical assumption during a
style edit. Preserve exact quotations and identifiers even when they contain a
catalogue phrase; note a material exception briefly.

Return the revised text with the supplied passage IDs, plus a short list of
unresolved findings and material exceptions. The coordinator removes review IDs
from user-facing prose and is the only writer to the user's files.

On receiving recheck findings, make one local correction pass. Inspect your own
replacement and compare its meaning again. Do not broaden into general polishing
or request further rounds. Preserve unresolved items in the final report. The
last repairs have a self-check, not another independent detector check.
