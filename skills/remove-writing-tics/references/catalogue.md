## Pattern guide

| Habit | Look for | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manufactured contrast | Habitual "not X, but Y", "less X than Y", correction of an alternative the reader never proposed | 1–12, 104, 112, 166, 201, 224 |
| Announced importance | Crucially, notably, importantly, subtle-but-consequential claims without stating the consequence | 15, 20, 91, 111, 143, 202–205, 214, 263 |
| Throat-clearing | Announcements of explanation, unpacking, pausing, stepping back, or being concrete | 13–25, 105, 118, 157, 180 |
| Performed honesty or care | Narration of candor, caution, restraint, verification, or thoughtful work | 103, 110, 121–124, 138–139, 146, 183, 187, 192, 199 |
| Stock editorial metaphors | Words earning places, claims carrying weight, words doing work, architectural or textural metaphors used as criticism | 101–102, 107, 137, 140, 170–171, 184, 194 |
| Slogans and maxims | Clipped verdicts, moralized writing advice, bumper-sticker engineering wisdom | 115–116, 145, 150, 159, 162, 164, 172, 188–190, 195 |
| False depth or literary decoration | Quiet power, deeper meanings, spaces between abstractions, staged revelation | 26–35, 113, 120, 131, 179, 186 |
| Generic praise and reassurance | Groundbreaking, intuitive, seamless, principled, fair point, good instinct | 36–50, 109, 135, 163, 174, 197–198, 206, 299 |
| Bureaucratic abstraction | Nominalizations, concealed agency, inflated verbs, procedural filler | 51–65 |
| Vague qualification | Stacked hedges, generic balance, noncommittal conclusions, unspecific caution | 66–80, 169–170, 244, 249 |
| Technical self-praise | By-design claims, thin wrappers, explicit boundaries, robust frameworks, guarantees exceeding stated evidence | 218–243, 265, 275–291 |
| Academic scaffolding | Gap-filling announcements, implications without particulars, future-work formulas, "straightforward" extensions | 204–215, 246–274, 296–300 |
| Automatic endings | Takeaways, final maxims, hopeful forecasts, unrequested offers to continue | 93–100, 156, 200, 299–300 |
| Repeated cadence and punctuation | Rules of three, three-adjective conclusions, em-dash dependence, identical openings or contrast templates | 145, 152–153, 165, 193, 299 |

Also inspect stock vocabulary such as "delve", "tapestry", "nuanced", and
"load-bearing", including paraphrases. A necessary contrast, factual condition,
or uncertainty statement must keep its meaning through revision. Do not replace
technical terms merely because a catalogue specimen also uses them. When the
phrase itself is explicitly unwanted, revise it rather than defending it as
acceptable writing in general.
