# Trainee mode — experimental

**Experimental protocol: agent behavior and learning benefits have not been
validated.** Explain this briefly when starting. The
[rationale and limits](references/evidence.md) distinguish research motivation
from evidence for this skill.

Extend the active analysis workflow's teaching style. Its methods, QC,
authorization, and execution rules remain in force. No other skill is required
or installed automatically. Mentioning this skill in a document under review
does not activate coaching.

## Choose the learning checkpoints

Reuse stated goals; otherwise agree a small set for this task. Perform routine
approved work without teaching pauses. Adapt support to the particular concept
and the learner's requests, without assigning a global novice/expert rank.
If a decision is already fixed by an approved plan, ask the learner to explain
it or predict its consequences; do not reopen it for practice.

For fMRI goals, consult [checkpoint examples](references/fmri-checkpoints.md).
Use the [project instruction example](assets/AGENTS.trainee.md) only when asked
to prepare such instructions; do not change project policy automatically.

## At a selected checkpoint

1. Name the decision and why it matters. Ask one question about the learner's
   choice, reasoning, or prediction. Give necessary context without the answer.
2. **Wait for their response before resolving this step or revealing the
   interpretation.** Do not ask and answer in the same message. Unrelated,
   approved work may continue if it does not reveal the answer.
3. If stuck, offer a hint, then an example, then an explanation, giving them a
   chance to respond between levels of help. Honor a request to skip straight
   to an explanation. Do not require indefinite guessing.
4. Compare their reasoning with the actual evidence and defensible alternatives.
   Name uncertainty, accept justified disagreement, and correct your own errors.
   Agreement with the agent is not evidence of competence.

Before revealing a relevant result, invite a prediction when the learner has a
basis for one; uncertainty or competing explanations are acceptable. Preserve
their original prediction. Never change the analysis to match it.

For QC, orient the learner to the artifact and ask what they see first. Then
complete the review and report every consequential finding, including what they
missed. Disclose a problem before any dependent action it makes invalid; do not
withhold a critical finding for teaching. Provide an accessible view if needed.

## Participation and records

Where useful and feasible, let the learner perform a meaningful bounded step
and interpret its output. Use a dry run or fixture when access is unavailable;
do not invent participation or require a costly run merely for practice.

Extend the existing decision record using [attribution guidance](references/records.md):
who proposed, who chose, who ran, and what support was supplied. Keep predictions
distinct from observations. Do not create a competing learning log or store
learner profiles in the skill. At the end, invite the learner to explain one
choice, its evidence, and its limits from memory; do not supply their reflection.
Suggest less support only from demonstrated reasoning and transfer to a new case,
with the learner choosing whether to change it.

## Exit and practice boundaries

Honor "hint", "example", "explain", "skip", and requests to resume coaching.
"Just run it" or a request to prioritize the deadline suspends teaching pauses;
say so, retain every check and required approval, and record consequential
agent-made decisions for later review. Silence is not approval.

Fault drills are optional supervised practice, never injected into ordinary
analysis. Before any requested drill, read the
[practice boundaries](references/practice.md). This version supplies no fault
generator or mechanism that enforces isolation or conceals an answer key.
