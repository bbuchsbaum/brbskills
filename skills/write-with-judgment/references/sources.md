Recheck exact conventions when an edition, venue, or disputed point makes currency material. Cite the actual source consulted; do not invent manual paragraph numbers.

## Strunk and White

**William Strunk Jr. and E. B. White, The Elements of Style, fourth edition.** Cited by chapter and principle number, which are stable across printings. The book is not bundled with this skill.

- Chapter II, principles 12–13: design the composition and give paragraphs coherent functions. The text allows different structures for different kinds of writing.
- Chapter II, principle 14: prefer active voice where useful; the explanation explicitly permits passive voice and relates voice choice to the sentence's topic.
- Chapter II, principle 17: remove unnecessary material without requiring every sentence to be short or every detail to disappear.
- Chapter II, principles 20 and 22: attend to the proximity of related expressions and the placement of emphasis.
- Chapter V, reminders 7–8 and 16: avoid overstatement, distinguish empty intensification from substance, and value clarity without denying literary purpose.

Apply these as composition advice. Do not inherit every historical usage judgment. The fourth edition's treatment of singular “they” in chapter IV, differs from current Chicago guidance. Its examples sometimes infer what a writer intended; this skill requires support before making that inference factual.

## Chicago: public primary guidance

The [official home page](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/home.html) identifies the eighteenth edition, copyright 2024. The references below are publicly accessible official pages, not a claim to have read the full subscription manual.

| Source | Verified guidance relevant here |
| --- | --- |
| [What's New in the 18th Edition](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/help-tools/what-s-new.html) | Generic singular “they” is an accepted option. Consistent title case or sentence case may be used for subheads. Several punctuation and citation conventions changed, so edition matters. |
| [Pronouns Q&A](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/Pronouns/faq0031.html) | Distinguishes generic singular “they” from its use for a specific person. Respect an individual's pronouns. |
| [Scientific-report passive-voice Q&A](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/Usage/faq0305.html) | Passive voice may be efficient when agency is understood; indiscriminate conversion can make scientific editing worse. Assess actual obscurity and awkwardness. |
| [Canadian-English Q&A](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/CMOS/faq0015.html) | Regional English can coexist with Chicago conventions; select an appropriate dictionary unless the publisher requires otherwise. |
| [Citation Quick Guide](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide.html) | Chicago offers notes-and-bibliography and author-date systems; use the system required by the field or publisher. |

Keep editorial conventions, English variety, and citation system separate. Preserve required journal style rather than converting all references to Chicago. Consult the relevant source for unusual punctuation, names, capitalization, numbers, quotations, or citations. If needed subscription guidance is unavailable, state the uncertainty or apply a clearly labelled house choice; do not claim verification.

Treat articles on Chicago's Shop Talk blog according to their stated authorship, date, and policy disclaimers. An editor's useful advice on an official blog is not automatically current manual policy. The local style-sheet workflow in this skill is an original practical design and does not rely on an asserted Chicago paragraph.

## ASD-STE100 and the reference skill

Use the [official ASD overview](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html) and [downloads page](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html) when actual STE requirements matter. Issue 9 was identified by the official site's indexed material as the January 2025 issue during research; direct full-standard access was unavailable. Do not infer that the full dictionary or every rule has been verified here. Obtain the applicable authorized standard for compliance work.

The conceptual influence is explicit action, stable terminology, and recoverable conditions. This skill does not impose STE's controlled vocabulary or sentence limits on general prose. It contains no official dictionary or reproduced standard.

An existing STE-based skill, [danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill](https://github.com/danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill), observed at commit `32511c6992ecb5f1971e46a2943f2e6adceedafe`, skill version 0.4.0, was the starting point for this skill's handling of agent-facing text. Its [SKILL.md](https://github.com/danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill/blob/32511c6992ecb5f1971e46a2943f2e6adceedafe/SKILL.md) already emphasizes modality preservation and acknowledges compliance limits. Its [examples](https://github.com/danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill/blob/32511c6992ecb5f1971e46a2943f2e6adceedafe/examples/before-after.md) and [linter](https://github.com/danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill/blob/32511c6992ecb5f1971e46a2943f2e6adceedafe/scripts/ste-lint.py) informed the decision to separate meaning review from surface diagnostics. This skill keeps its target reader (text parsed without a chance to ask) and its concerns, but replaces fixed caps and a failing linter with each concern's purpose and the conditions for an exception.

That repository uses an MIT license, copyright 2026 Dustin Yuchen Teng. This skill's prose, worked examples, and comparison utility were written independently; no repository code, book chapters, or official controlled dictionary is copied into the package.
